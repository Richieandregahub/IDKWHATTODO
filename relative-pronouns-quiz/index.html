The short version

PronounRefers toRole in the clauseExample
whopeoplesubjectThe tutor who helped me was patient.
whompeopleobject, or after a prepositionThe tutor whom I thanked; the tutor with whom I worked.
whosepeople & thingspossessiveThe tutor whose book I borrowed; a town whose streets are narrow.
whichthings; a whole clausesubject / objectThe book which I bought; he was late, which annoyed us.
thatpeople & thingssubject / objectThe book that I bought. Defining clauses only.
whatthe thing(s) thatno noun before itWhat she said surprised me.
when / wheretime / placerelative adverbsthe day when we met; the town where I grew up.

Three tests that answer almost every question

  1. 1Person or thing? People take who / whom. Things take which / that. Whose works for both.
  2. 2Subject or object? Rewrite the clause with he / him. “He helped me” → who. “I thanked him” → whom.
  3. 3Are there commas? Commas mean a non-defining clause: use which (never that), keep who / whom, and never drop the pronoun.

Fine print worth knowing

  1. •That is the natural choice after all, everything, nothing, anything, the only, the best — and it can never follow a preposition or a comma.
  2. •Which can refer to a whole idea: “Sales fell 20%, which worried the board.” That cannot do this.
  3. •Whom is formal. In conversation “who” is often used for objects, but exams and careful writing expect whom — especially after a preposition.
  4. •Omission: you may drop the relative pronoun only when it is the object. “The book (which) I bought” ✅ — “The man who called” ❌ cannot lose who.
  5. •What already contains its noun: don’t write “the thing what”. Use what alone or the thing that.
